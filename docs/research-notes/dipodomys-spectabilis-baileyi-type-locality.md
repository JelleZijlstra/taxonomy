# Where was the type locality of _Dipodomys spectabilis baileyi_?

_Jelle S. Zijlstra, September 2026_

The type locality of _Dipodomys spectabilis baileyi_ Goldman, 1923 was reported as 40
miles west of Roswell, New Mexico. However, later publications offer some contradictory
evidence and the exact position of the type locality has not been determined. A later
type catalogue identifies the collecting site as Cedar Point ranch. Bailey's field
notebooks connect that stop with the name Block Ranch; historical maps provide a
specific candidate, the former Block Ranch Camp, subsequently mapped as Armstrong Ranch.

## Type specimen and published locality accounts

[Goldman (1923:140)](/a/15126) described the name from “40 miles west of Roswell, New
Mexico.” The holotype is **USNM:MAMM:97185**, an adult male, skin and skull, collected
by **Vernon Bailey on 13 June 1899**, original number **6961**. Goldman's locality
statement does not specify a county.

[Fisher and Ludwig (2012:46)](/a/15513) retain the west-of-Roswell locality but add
“[FC: at Cedar Point ranch], [Lincoln County]”. Their introduction (p. 3) defines FC as
the collector's field catalog. Thus, Cedar Point is explicitly attributed to the field
catalog; Lincoln County is a separate editorial addition. The field-catalog entry itself
has not been inspected in this investigation.

[Bailey (1931:259)](/a/16602) instead gives 40 miles **northwest** of Roswell. His
distribution map (p. 249, fig. 44) also plots the type locality northwest of Roswell.
[Frey (1996:9)](/a/34097), in _Mammalian type localities in New Mexico_, draws attention
to this discrepancy. Her gazetteer (p. 20, locality 33) places the locality in Lincoln
County as “64.4 km NW Roswell (T6–7S, R18–20E)”. This is a broad area of six survey
townships, not a ranch identification. Her species account on p. 9 inserts Chaves
County, inconsistently with the gazetteer.

## Bailey's contemporary notebooks

Two digitized notebooks in the Smithsonian Institution Archives document the trip:

- In the narrative diary, the
  [12 June 1899 entry](https://www.biodiversitylibrary.org/page/54361262) describes
  leaving Roswell for Mount Capitan, watering at a ranch eight miles out, and reaching
  Cedar Point, a ranch forty miles out, where Bailey camped and set traps. The
  [13 June entry](https://www.biodiversitylibrary.org/page/54361263), headed “Cedar
  Point ranch to base of Mt. Capitan”, records catching a _Dipodomys spectabilis_, along
  with other mammals. He then continued up the gulch about twenty miles to ranches at
  the mountain's base, camping at an old ranch and spring whose name appears to read
  “Las Palas” (transcription uncertain).
- A second notebook, catalogued as _Field notes, Texas and California, April 3–November
  1, 1899_, includes the New Mexico journey. Its
  [12–13 June entries](https://www.biodiversitylibrary.org/page/48287043) record leaving
  Roswell at 8:30 a.m., reaching “Block ranch” at 7:40 p.m., and a barometric elevation
  reading of 5,500 feet. The next morning's heading is “June 13. Block Ranch”, with a
  camp reading of 5,450 feet.

## Historical maps and gazetteer records

The
[USGS Arabela map of 1936](https://prd-tnm.s3.amazonaws.com/StagedProducts/Maps/HistoricalTopo/PDF/NM/48000/NM_Arabela_189604_1936_48000_geo.pdf)
labels **Block Ranch Camp** beside the old Roswell road. The
[USGS Arroyo Serrano West map of 1981](https://prd-tnm.s3.amazonaws.com/StagedProducts/Maps/HistoricalTopo/PDF/NM/24000/NM_Arroyo%20Serrano%20West_189640_1981_24000_geo.pdf)
labels **Armstrong Ranch** at the corresponding site, in **section 31, T7S, R18E**.

The
[GeoNames-derived gazetteer entry for Armstrong Ranch](https://mapcarta.com/23257144)
lists “Black Ranch” and “Block Ranch” as alternate names (GeoNames ID 5455733). A
[GNIS-derived listing](https://www.anyplaceamerica.com/directory/nm/lincoln-county-35027/locales/armstrong-ranch-903926/)
gives **33.6648074° N, 105.203042° W**, approximately 5,426 feet elevation, in Lincoln
County.

There is also a distinct
[Block Ranch site farther west](https://www.anyplaceamerica.com/directory/nm/lincoln-county-35027/locales/block-ranch-903927/),
near 33.712° N, 105.416° W. This is 56 miles from Roswell and at about 5942 feet, so it
is a less likely candidate for Bailey's collecting site. It is possible that Block Ranch
was a larger operation, with "Block Ranch camp" where Bailey collected as one of its
locations.

## Assessment

The evidence chain is **holotype → Cedar Point ranch → Bailey's Block Ranch stop →
mapped Block Ranch Camp → Armstrong Ranch**. Its links have different strengths:

1. The type catalogue explicitly ties the holotype to Cedar Point, citing the field
   catalog. The diary independently documents collecting _D. spectabilis_ there on the
   holotype's collection date.
2. The matching dates and itinerary in the two notebooks strongly suggest that Cedar
   Point and the stop called Block Ranch were the same overnight camp.
3. The map comparison and retained alternate name connect the mapped Block Ranch Camp
   with Armstrong Ranch. However, no source examined explicitly equates that mapped camp
   with **Cedar Point**.

Armstrong Ranch is therefore the best specific candidate found, approximately **33.6648°
N, 105.2030° W**. It falls within [Frey's (1996:20)](/a/34097) township range, and its
elevation is compatible with Bailey's barometric readings. A calculation from central
Roswell places it about 43 miles west-northwest in a straight line; this is only a rough
correspondence with the historical forty-mile journey.

Further archival research may strengthen the link. The
[Block Ranch Records at Texas Tech](https://txarchives.org/ttusw/finding_aids/02626.xml)
(collection S 1262.2) include New Mexico maps in box 1, folder 16, and oversized maps in
box 3, folder 1.
